- Teams: invitations and new passwords by email (the team's own mail server, SMTP with TLS), a "Forgot your password?" link on the sign-in page, and the person's name and email read from the directory at their first sign-in. A directory whose certificate the organization's own authority signed works with that authority's certificate.
- Teams: what a team's policy sets shows in the app as set by the team, and cannot be changed there. A new policy, "Incoming only", makes the app on those computers show the computer alone, its ID and how people reach it, for the people who are helped. A team can show its own name and logo as the app's, in the window's title and the task bar.
- Teams: single sign-on links only an email address the identity provider says it verified, and never takes over an account that has a password, two-step sign-in or an administrator's rights; a second join with an enrollment token cannot take a joined computer's place; the team server's data folder opens to administrators alone on Windows; and more from a security review (sign-in limits by account and network, one-time codes, the audit log of file transfers, CSV quoting).
- Linux: the app is in the applications menu once its window has opened, and `.deb` and `.rpm` packages come with each release (x86_64 and Arm64). `sshremote://` links open the app on Linux, Windows and macOS.
- Windows: `scoop bucket add sshremote https://github.com/sshremote-dev/scoop-bucket; scoop install sshremote`. A copy Homebrew, Scoop, winget or a package installed is updated by them: the app names the command.

Install with one line (`irm https://sshremote.dev/install.ps1 | iex` in PowerShell, `curl -fsSL https://sshremote.dev/install.sh | sh` on macOS and Linux), or download the file for your system below. An installed copy updates itself with `sshremote update`. `SHA256SUMS` lists every file's SHA-256, as does the signed [list](https://sshremote.dev/download/latest.json) the program updates from.
