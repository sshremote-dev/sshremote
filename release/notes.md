- Teams, for organizations: a team server of your own (`sshremote team serve`, behind your HTTPS) with a web console. Computers join with an enrollment token or the team's own installer line; people sign in with a password and an authenticator code, through your identity provider (OpenID Connect) or your directory (LDAP). The console sets who may reach which computers and with what rights, the policy each group of computers keeps (team members only or passwords too, asking first, rights, recording, idle end, straight connections, the local network), the team's name, logo and relay, and shows the audit log of every session, also as CSV.
- A signed-in person opens a team computer with no password: the team server gives a ticket for five minutes, bound to that person's own device, and the computer checks it with a key only it and the server hold. In the app: Settings, Team, and the team's computers in the home window's list; on the command line `sshremote team login` and `sshremote sshremote://ID --team`.
- Your own list of computers can be kept on the team server, sealed on your device with a passphrase the server never sees.

Install with one line (`irm https://sshremote.dev/install.ps1 | iex` in PowerShell, `curl -fsSL https://sshremote.dev/install.sh | sh` on macOS and Linux), or download the file for your system below. An installed copy updates itself with `sshremote update`. `SHA256SUMS` lists every file's SHA-256, as does the signed [list](https://sshremote.dev/download/latest.json) the program updates from.
